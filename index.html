UK Young Ambassadors

Young people’s priorities for the Commonwealth

We are gathering evidence from young people across the four nations of the UK on what is failing them now, what matters most for their future, and what they want government to bring back. These findings will be utilised to formulate policy for the next Commonwealth Youth Forum, taking place between the 2nd–4th November 2026 at the University of the West Indies, Five Islands Campus in Antigua & Barbuda. This event will take place within the larger Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting.

Why we are running this consultation

The Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting takes place bi-annually and is the largest gathering to set Commonwealth-wide policy. With more than half of the Commonwealth’s population being under 30, youth policy is a vital pillar of this agenda. These results will be fed into the lobbying and advocacy efforts presented in November.

1

To gather a national mandate

This consultation collects responses from young people across the four nations of the UK. The results provide the delegation with a measurable mandate to present to government and to Commonwealth institutions.

2

To connect domestic and international policy

Young people in the UK raise many of the same policy concerns as young people across the Commonwealth. This survey asks about both, so that domestic and Commonwealth priorities can be compared directly.

3

To produce a published record

Every response contributes to a published set of findings. The findings will be used by the delegation, by Youth Council UK and by the organisations taking this work forward.

Who we are

The UK Young Ambassadors (UKYA) represent the voices of young people from across the UK in European and Commonwealth institutes, policy places, and forums. The Young Ambassadors are elected in by youth organisations to represent UK young people and serve a term of three years.

Our wider work

The delegation is part of the UK Young Ambassadors programme run by the National Youth Agency, and works alongside Youth Council UK, the national youth voice structure spanning the four nations. Between us we have represented young people at the Commonwealth Youth Forum, at CHOGM, and in conversations with UK departments about the policies that affect young people at home.

What we are asking from you

Five sections that ask participants to rank policy priority areas, informing the initiatives that will be undertaken by Youth Council UK and the Commonwealth Youth Forum.

Bring it back

Participants select up to three areas of provision that they want government to reintroduce for young people. The list includes Education Maintenance Allowance, funded youth clubs, school counsellors and capped bus fares.

The biggest problem

Participants identify the single biggest problem facing young people in the UK, and the single biggest problem facing young people across the Commonwealth. Each answer is given as a selected policy area and in the words of the respondent.

What is working

Participants score each policy area out of ten for how well it currently works for young people. Low scores indicate where delivery is failing rather than where interest is lowest.

What matters, and when

Participants rate the same twelve policy areas twice, for long term importance and for short term urgency. The difference between the two scores identifies where action is most needed.

What happens to your answers

1

You respond

Anonymously, through a Google Form. No name or email is required to take part.

2

Results go live

Aggregated answers will appear on the results page and updated after every response.

3

Analysis of the Findings

The results are analysed and written up into findings and recommendations.

4

We take it in

To UK departments, to Commonwealth youth structures and into our delegation’s position at CHOGM.

Responses are anonymous and will be held for 24 months. Free-text answers may be quoted in the report without any detail that could identify the individual. Read the privacy notice.
