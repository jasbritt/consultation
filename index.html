UK Young Ambassadors

Young people’s priorities for the Commonwealth

We are gathering evidence from young people across the four nations of the UK on what is failing them now, what matters most for their future, and what they want government to bring back. These findings will be utilised to formulate policy for the next Commonwealth Youth Forum, taking place between the 2nd–4th November 2026 at the University of the West Indies, Five Islands Campus in Antigua & Barbuda. This event will take place within the larger Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting.

Why we are running this consultation

The Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting takes place bi-annually and is the largest gathering to set Commonwealth-wide policy. With more than half of the Commonwealth’s population being under 30, youth policy is a vital pillar of this agenda. These results will be fed into the lobbying and advocacy efforts presented in November.

1

To take a real mandate, not an opinion

We are asked constantly what “young people think”. This consultation lets us answer with a sample, a method and a margin of error rather than an anecdote — and lets anyone check our working on the results page.

2

To join up domestic and international policy

The problems young people raise at home — mental health waits, transport costs, insecure work — are the same problems raised across the Commonwealth. We ask about both in the same survey so the connection is evidenced rather than asserted.

3

To leave something behind

Every response feeds a published report with the underlying numbers attached. It is written to be usable by departments, by youth organisations and by the next delegation, not just by us.

Who we are

The UK Young Ambassadors (UKYA) represent the voices of young people from across the UK in European and Commonwealth institutes, policy places, and forums. The Young Ambassadors are elected in by youth organisations to represent UK young people and serve a term of three years.

Our wider work

The delegation is part of the UK Young Ambassadors programme run by the National Youth Agency, and works alongside Youth Council UK, the national youth voice structure spanning the four nations. Between us we have represented young people at the Commonwealth Youth Forum, at CHOGM, and in conversations with UK departments about the policies that affect young people at home.

What we are asking from you

Five short sections. The rating questions use the policy priority areas carried by the British Youth Council and the UK Youth Parliament, so the answers can be compared against a decade of existing youth-voice evidence rather than starting from scratch.

Bring it back

The top three things you want government to reintroduce for young people — from Education Maintenance Allowance to youth clubs, school counsellors and capped bus fares.

The biggest problem

What you think is the single biggest problem facing young people in the UK today, and the single biggest problem facing young people across the Commonwealth — in your own words as well as from a list.

What is working

A score out of ten for how well each policy area is currently working for young people you know. Low scores tell us where delivery is failing, not just where interest lies.

What matters, and when

The same twelve areas rated twice: how important each is to your long-term future, and how urgent each is over the next twelve months. The distance between the two is where the report begins.

What happens to your answers

1

You respond

Anonymously, through a Google Form. No name or email is required to take part.

2

Results go live

Aggregated answers appear on the results page — maps, ratings and the priority gap — updated as responses arrive.

3

We write it up

We turn the data into findings and recommendations, with the numbers attached so they can be checked.

4

We take it in

To UK departments, to Commonwealth youth structures and into our delegation’s position at CHOGM.

Responses are anonymous, held for 24 months, and reported only in aggregate. Free-text answers may be quoted in the report without any identifying detail. Read the privacy notice.
